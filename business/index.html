New bank details on an invoice? Check before you pay.

Fake invoices and “urgent payment” emails from someone pretending to be your boss or supplier are one of the costliest scams for UK businesses. Paste the email or invoice text below, or add a screenshot, and get a plain answer in seconds. Free.

£138m lost by UK individuals and businesses to business email compromise in 12 months, about £30,000 per case, according to the National Economic Crime Centre.

Red flags in business payment scams

  • A supplier says their bank details have changed, by email only
  • The sender's email address is slightly different from usual (a missing letter, .co instead of .co.uk)
  • Urgency or secrecy: “pay today”, “keep this between us”
  • “The boss” asks for a quick transfer or gift cards while they're unreachable
  • An invoice you weren't expecting, or a new payee name

What to do before you pay

  1. Call the supplier or colleague on a number you already have, not one in the email, to confirm any change of bank details.
  2. Use Confirmation of Payee when you add a new payee, and pause if the name doesn't match.
  3. Make it a rule that new or changed bank details need a second person to approve.
  4. If you've already paid, call your bank straight away, then report it to Report Fraud at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040.
